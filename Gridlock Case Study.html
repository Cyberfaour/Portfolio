CASE STUDY · 04
◆ Gridlock · Enterprise Utility ERP Platform

An ERP where no business policy is hardcoded.

Gridlock is a configurable, enterprise-grade platform for the full lifecycle of a gas utility — metering & billing, work orders, field ops, assets, compliance, procurement, and a self-service customer portal. Built on one principle: it starts empty and is configured by business admins, not engineers. Every workflow, approval chain, SLA threshold and document template is admin-editable data.

01 — THE THESIS

Most utility ERPs bake business rules into code. So every policy change needs an engineer.

A new approval threshold, a different billing cadence, a new document type — each becomes a developer ticket, a release, and downtime. Gridlock inverts that: business policy is data, configured through the UI, with no JSON editing, no GUID hunting, and no app restarts.

02 — CONFIGURABLE ENGINES

The platform is a set of engines — all admin-managed, all empty on first boot.

Instead of hardcoded behaviors scattered through the codebase, each policy concern is its own engine with a configuration surface. A /admin/setup Configuration Health dashboard shows which engines are configured versus empty, and configuration bundles export/import with SHA-256 integrity checks and dry-run diffs.

Sequence / numbering

Fail-closed document numbering for invoices, work orders, contracts.

Approval chains

Visual, condition-based multi-step approvals — no seeded thresholds.

Notification rules

Routing, templating, channel and dedupe policy as data, not config files.

Workflow engine

Status-as-workflow-nodes; admin-composed lifecycle definitions.

Work-order scheduler

Policy-driven automation — cadence, lookahead, retries — replacing hardcoded pollers.

SLA & visibility

SLA policies and row-level security expressed as rules, not branches.

Entity timeline & links

Append-only audit spine plus cross-record relationships.

ServiceDocument

QuestPDF generation — QR-verified, branded, signature-captured controlled docs.

Config health & transfer

Setup dashboard plus export/import with SHA-256 integrity & dry-run diffs.

01 · Modules talk only via contracts

Cross-module communication happens through Contracts — interfaces, DTOs, enums — or domain events, never direct references. The boundaries are real, not conventional.

02 · Application slices, not service soup

Razor pages reach the domain only through permissioned MediatR query/command handlers — never by injecting services or DbContext.

03 · Event-driven over manual buttons

Schedule, threshold and state-transition triggers replace force-run actions, which are confined to hidden diagnostics surfaces. SignalR powers the live operational surfaces.

05 — ENGINEERING DISCIPLINE

The project enforces its own doctrine — programmatically.

1,460 methods
SURFACE-AUDIT TOOL

Every service method across 267 interfaces is tracked against its UI exposure — coverage stays classified, dead surface area can't accumulate.

0 exceptions
GUARDRAIL TESTS

Tests lock in invariants: no hardcoded policy, no manual-refresh buttons on user surfaces, no cross-module leakage, MediatR-only page access.

9+8 increments
WAVE-BASED PROGRAM

Phases 1–9, then Waves 0–7 — each documented with an architecture doc, an audit snapshot, and dedicated guardrail tests.

Work was driven by a systematic trim-and-consolidate mandate: collapsing duplicate surfaces, removing legacy shims, and converting hardcoded behavior into configurable engines — increment after increment.

06 — OUTCOME

A utility ERP a non-technical administrator can stand up and fully configure — workflows, approvals, documents, billing rules — without a developer, while a guardrail-enforced architecture keeps the codebase consistent as it scales across every domain.
